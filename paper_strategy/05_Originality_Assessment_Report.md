### 300-word justification

QIQT-H's foundations paper targets the unsolved measurement problem with a framework that combines three established but disconnected research lines: informational axiomatizations of quantum theory (Hardy 2001 and successors), holographic bounds on physical information (Bekenstein 1981; Bousso 2002; Banks 2025), and finite-information collapse mechanisms (Srikanth 2003 and several follow-ups). No prior work fuses these. The proposal's principal claim — that a finite, locally varying coherent-information capacity `Q_R`, grounded in the Wald entropy of a bounding surface, suffices to produce single measurement outcomes from otherwise unitary microevolution — sits in a genuine architectural gap mapped in Gaps 1–3 of the gap analysis.

Three factors temper the impact estimate. First, Srikanth (2003) already proposed that exhausting a finite Hilbert-space "fine-graining" forces an "information transition" essentially equivalent to collapse; QIQT-H's novelty hinges on its *geometric grounding* of the bound (Wald, area-law) rather than treating the bound as primitive. Reviewers will measure the paper against Srikanth, not against the standard MWI/CSL alternatives. Second, the most direct empirical signature — modified collapse rate scaling with effective dimension — has to clear current LISA-Pathfinder and X-ray-emission bounds on CSL, which are tight; the QIQT-H paper must propose at least one falsifiable prediction that is not already excluded. Third, Palmer's 2025 RaQM occupies an adjacent slot in the same year and may attract the audience QIQT-H is courting.

If the paper engages Srikanth with rigor, provides at least one quantitative falsifiable prediction (e.g., a coherence-bound for nested-observer experiments or a Q-dependent neutrino-oscillation correction at the energies where IceCube already constrains decoherence), and explicitly positions QIQT-H as a stratum *above* Palmer's RaQM (RaQM as candidate microtheory, QIQT-H as the EFT that makes the measurement-problem connection), the work has solid potential for a placement among the noticeable 2026 quant-ph foundations preprints. **Estimated impact range: 7–8/10**, contingent on differentiation rigor.
