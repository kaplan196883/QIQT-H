## What the repairs did NOT fix — four new technical objections

### New objection 1 — 𝒞_Q is FINITE, not countable (real math error)

> "For a fixed universal Turing machine and fixed Q, there are only finitely many programs of length ≤ Q. Therefore the class 𝒞_Q is finite, not countable. The abstract and summary say 𝒞_Q is countable and includes algebraic numbers such as cos²(π/n) and transcendentals such as 1-e^(-1). But for fixed Q, it cannot include all such numbers. It can include only those computable by programs within the fixed length bound."

This is correct. I made a math error in the repair pass. To include cos²(π/n) for all n, you need programs whose length grows with n. Fixed Q gives finitely many programs, hence finitely many computable reals.

The paper's central new formulation is technically wrong as stated.

### New objection 2 — Proposition 6 fails in BOTH directions

The central conditional necessity result. GPT-5.5 gives explicit counterexamples:

**Necessity fails:**
> "Consider a two-outcome measurement with projectors P and I-P, and a state such that ⟨Ψ|P|Ψ⟩ = p, where p is a noncomputable real. The Boolean algebra generated by {P, I-P} is finite. Thus finite record structure does not require computable Born weights."

**Sufficiency fails:**
> "A restriction on the numerical complexity of Born weights does not by itself imply that the set of decohered macroscopic projectors D_t is finite ... Finiteness of R_t depends on the number and organization of record-projectors, not merely on the computability of their probabilities."

GPT-5.5's conclusion:
> "The only way Proposition 6 becomes true is if 'well-defined finite record algebra' is defined to include (FQ) as a precondition. But then the result becomes definitional rather than substantive."

This is the most serious finding. Proposition 6 — which I made the *central structural result of the entire paper* — is mathematically incorrect as stated.

### New objection 3 — Theorem 1 is a conservative-extension triviality

> "Theorem 1 — that T_QM ⊬ φ_i and T_QM ⊬ ¬φ_i — is not a deep model-theoretic result. It follows because the language has been expanded by adding a World sort and a valuation predicate v, while the axioms of T_QM constrain only the Born-weight structure. If a predicate is introduced and left unconstrained, then sentences involving that predicate will normally be independent. That is a conservative-extension observation, not a substantive theorem about quantum mechanics."

Also correct. I introduced an unconstrained valuation `v` and then proved independence of statements involving `v`. That's by construction, not by discovery.

### New objection 4 — Theorem 5 doesn't depend on (FQ)

> "Theorem 5 ... is mathematically standard: a product of finite discrete compact spaces is compact by Tychonoff ... But this result does not depend on (FQ). It depends on the finiteness of the outcome sets O_n. If those sets are finite, compactness follows regardless of whether Born weights are computable, noncomputable, algebraic, transcendental, or unspecified."

Also correct. The compactness theorem I reformulated in the repair pass uses König's lemma on finite branching, which doesn't require (FQ).

## Additional issues identified

### Underdeveloped actuality structure

> "A single valuation at each time is not enough. The paper needs an account of: (1) Temporal coherence, (2) Conditionalization, (3) Quantum temporal correlations, (4) Context selection, (5) Relativistic setting, (6) Wigner's friend / nested observers."

The Boolean valuation device handles single-time outcomes but not the temporal structure of quantum theory.

### Modal interpretation distinction unclear

> "The paper distinguishes itself from modal interpretations by saying it does not add a global modal preferred-property rule. But it does add a rule selecting decohered macroscopic record-projectors as the privileged domain of valuation. That is at least modal-adjacent. The difference needs sharper articulation."

### Q global vs local tension

> "There is also tension between a universal constant Q in (FQ) and a region-dependent holographic quantity Q_R = A(∂R)/(4ℓ_P² ln 2). Is Q global, local, time-dependent, region-dependent, observer-dependent, or context-dependent? This matters greatly."

### Holographic discussion overstated

> "The Bekenstein/Bousso bounds concern entropy or information capacity ... They do not directly entail a global finite upper bound Q on the description length of all physically realizable Born probabilities."

(I acknowledged this in the repair pass — but the paper still treats the holographic identification as more load-bearing than it is.)

## What revision would require

GPT-5.5's recommended fix list (substantial):

1. **Correct (FQ)** — decide between computability, bounded Kolmogorov complexity, finite operational resolution, or holographic finite dimensionality. The current formulation conflates them.
2. **Fix the 𝒞_Q error** — for fixed Q, the set is finite, not countable.
3. **Withdraw or radically reformulate Proposition 6** — the iff claim is mathematically incorrect.
4. **Clarify Theorem 1 as conservative-extension/free-predicate observation** — not as a substantive theorem.
5. **Define the record algebra rigorously** — what counts as a decohered macroscopic projector? What selects a context?
6. **Add temporal and probabilistic structure** — valuations at single times are insufficient.
7. **Clarify hidden-variable status** — primitive actual-world valuation IS an additional beable/actuality variable.
8. **Keep holographic discussion modest** — it motivates but doesn't derive (FQ).

GPT-5.5's recasting of the publishable version:

> "The promising paper here is not 'we prove that (FQ) is the necessary and sufficient bridge for minimalist single-world quantum mechanics.' That claim is not established. The promising paper is rather: 'A single-world no-collapse interpretation can be formulated by distinguishing Born weights from actuality valuations over finite contextual record algebras; here is the metaphysical cost and the relation to modal/decoherence-based interpretations.' That would be a more defensible and potentially publishable contribution."
