# QIQT-H — One-paragraph thesis summary

*Two versions of the same thesis. The first is the referee-facing version
(GPT-5.5-pro–endorsed, 2026-06-01, after applying its 11 wording softeners —
guards against strong-monism, fundamental-probability, "dissolves", literal-
binarity, and "any bounded region" overclaims). The second is a plain-language
variant that keeps the same content but drops the technical term "Parameter
Independence" for a general reader. Both state the same physics.*

---

## A. Referee-facing version (endorsed)

QIQT-H proposes that quantum mechanics needs no collapse, no extra particles,
no branching worlds, and no modified dynamics — only an honest accounting of two
structural facts, together with one non-dynamical actuality fact. First, there
is one wave function Φ, evolving by one exactly-unitary law, and it carries no
primitive single-run chance: Born weights are to be recovered as
frequencies/typicalities *across* many runs, not postulated as fundamental
chances for individual runs. Second, taking the Bekenstein–Bousso (covariant
entropy) bound as a literal physical constraint, any finite-area causal diamond
can instantiate only finitely many operationally distinguishable records. The
apparent measurement problem is then *decomposed*, not by adding a mechanism but
by removing a mistake — the assumption that the definite, either/or outcomes we
see are a feature of the wave function. They are not: experienced definiteness
lives at the level of actual macroscopic record-structures, which decoherence and
einselection stabilize into robust, effectively classical, coarse-grained pointer
records — *without by themselves selecting a unique one*. We register outcomes as
discrete, effectively classical alternatives, often encoded as 0/1, because
observers, detectors, and brains are themselves finite macroscopic
record-structures inside Φ, not observers standing outside it. The framework is
therefore scrupulous about a division of labor: decoherence stabilizes
effectively classical records (robustness, not uniqueness); the holographic bound
makes the set of distinguishable records finite (cardinality, not selection); and
a single non-dynamical, atemporal fact λ — which of Φ's macroscopic realizations
the whole run actually is — supplies *which* record is real. So the complete
description of a run is the pair (Φ, λ): Φ the sole *dynamical* ontology, λ a
non-dynamical actuality fact (no force, no second dynamical substance, no
intrinsic chance or sampling law, no guidance law). It is honest about what
remains open: a canonical measure over λ that — with measurement independence
retained and the Bell-required ontic violation of Parameter Independence allowed
— recovers Born statistics and operational no-signaling without fine-tuning is
not yet constructed; and the stronger claim that finite record-capacity *forbids*
the simultaneous instantiation of macroscopically distinct record-realizations
(which would let the holographic bound "select" the one world outright, rather
than merely bound it) is a conjecture, currently unproven. In one line: there is
only the unitary wave function as dynamical ontology, plus an atemporal fact
about which macroscopic record-realization is actual — the discrete, definite,
probabilistic appearance is a texture of our scale, not of the fundamental
dynamics.

## B. Plain-language version (no jargon)

QIQT-H says quantum mechanics needs no wave-function collapse, no hidden
particles, no parallel worlds, and no change to the equations — only an honest
look at two facts about the world, plus one extra fact about what is actually the
case. First fact: there is just one wave function, evolving by one exact,
unbroken law, and it has no built-in probability. The famous "probabilities" of
quantum mechanics are simply how often each result *turns up across many repeats*
of an experiment — never a chance attached to a single run. Second fact:
information is limited by surface area (the holographic bound from black-hole
physics, taken literally), so any finite region of space can hold only a finite
number of *tellable-apart* records. With these two facts, the measurement problem
stops being a paradox — not because we added a trick, but because we dropped a
wrong assumption: that the sharp either/or outcomes we see are built into the
wave function. They aren't. Sharpness belongs to *large, everyday record-keeping
structures* — pointer needles, detector clicks, ink on paper, neurons — which
ordinary decoherence makes stable, rugged, and effectively classical. We see
results as definite "this or that" because *we ourselves* — our instruments, our
brains — are large structures made of the wave function, not observers watching it
from outside. The labor divides cleanly: decoherence makes records stable and
classical (it does not pick a winner); the area limit makes the number of
possible records finite (it counts, it does not pick); and one further,
non-dynamical fact — call it λ, the fact of *which* of the wave function's
possible large-scale versions is the one actually happening — picks the single
result. So a run of the world is fully described by the pair (wave function, λ):
the wave function is the only thing that evolves and obeys a law, and λ is just
the bare fact of which version is real — not a substance, not a force, not a
probability, not a guiding hand. The program is candid about what is *not* yet
done: it does not yet have a derivation of the exact quantum statistics from λ
that also guarantees you can't use the setup to send faster-than-light signals
(while keeping experimenters genuinely free to choose what to measure); and the
bolder claim that the area limit *forbids* large-scale superpositions outright —
which would let surface area, by itself, pick out the one world — is so far a
conjecture, not a theorem. In one sentence: there is only the wave function and a
fact about which of its large-scale versions is actual — the sharp, definite,
seemingly-random world we experience is a feature of our scale, not of reality's
underlying machinery.
